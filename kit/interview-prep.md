Common interview questions with plain-English guidance on how to answer.

## The STAR method (for stories)

When they ask about a time you did something, use STAR:

- **S**ituation – What was happening?
- **T**ask – What did you need to do?
- **A**ction – What did *you* do (not the team)?
- **R**esult – What happened? Use numbers if you can.

Example: "A patient was confused about their discharge meds (S). I needed to
make sure they understood before leaving (T). I sat down with them, went
through each medication, and gave them a written checklist (A). They could
repeat the plan back to me, and we avoided a return visit (R)."

## Questions they might ask you

### Warm-up

1. **Tell me about yourself.** Keep it to 60 seconds: present, past, and why
   you're here. "I'm a registered nurse with four years on a telemetry unit,
   and I'm looking for a clinic role with steadier hours."
2. **Why do you want this job?** Mention something specific about the employer
   and one thing you'd bring.
3. **Why are you leaving your current role?** Stay positive. Focus on what
   you're moving toward, not what you're escaping.

### Experience and skills

4. **What's your greatest strength?** Pick one that matters for this job, then
   give a short example.
5. **What's your greatest weakness?** Pick something real you're improving,
   and say how. "I used to stay late finishing charts. I now block charting
   time during shifts, and I'm on time nearly every day."
6. **Describe a time you handled a difficult patient or coworker.** Use STAR.
   Focus on staying calm and fixing the problem, not on blame.
7. **Tell me about a time you made a mistake.** Own it, say what you did to
   fix it, and what you'd do differently next time.

### Scenario questions (healthcare)

8. **What would you do if you noticed a medication error?** Pause, check the
   patient, follow your facility's policy, report it, and document.
9. **How do you prioritize when everything feels urgent?** Safety first, then
   time-sensitive needs, then everything else. Ask for help when you need it.
10. **How do you explain something complicated to a patient?** Use plain
    language, check that they understand, and offer written instructions.

### Questions to ask them

Always prepare 2–3. Good options:

- What does success look like in this role in the first 90 days?
- Can you tell me about the team I'd be working with?
- What's onboarding and training like for new hires?
- What do you enjoy most about working here?
- What are the next steps in the hiring process?

## After the interview

Send a short thank-you email within 24 hours (see `kit/networking-emails.md`
for a script). Mention one thing you talked about and restate your interest.
